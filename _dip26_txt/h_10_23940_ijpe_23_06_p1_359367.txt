Abstract 
Production companies aim to achieve several strategic objectives to remain competitive in the market, including increased productivity, 
improved profitability, and reduced operational costs. One effective approach to reducing operational costs is implementing a preventive 
maintenance optimization. This study explores three optimization strategies for reducing maintenance costs: the age replacement model, 
optimal inspection model, and optimization via Design of Experiment (DOE). The first strategy involves simulating the equipment's aging 
process and scheduling replacement when it reaches a certain age or usage level. In the second strategy, the goal is to identify the optimal 
timing for maintenance inspections, which minimizes costs while ensuring equipment reliability. The third strategy involves developing a 
mathematical model to minimize the expected cost by using ANOVA analysis and response surface methodology to identify the optimal 
parameter values. The study assesses the efficiency of the three optimization strategies in reducing costs, using the Weibull distribution 
function as the basis for cost reduction evaluation. The results indicate that shape parameter and replacement time significantly impact the 
expected cost. Hence, production companies can utilize these findings to determine the most efficient and cost-effective preventive 
maintenance strategy for their equipment. 
 
Keywords: preventive maintenance; optimization; design of experiment; age replacement; inspection model

1.  Introduction  
 
Production companies aim to maintain their competitiveness in the market by meeting several requirements, including 
increased productivity, improved profitability, and reduced operational costs. One effective approach to reducing operational 
costs is implementing a preventive maintenance strategy. Preventive maintenance is a proactive maintenance approach that 
involves scheduling regular inspections, repairs, and replacement of equipment before failures occur, to prevent downtime 
and reduce costs associated with unplanned maintenance. However, implementing preventive maintenance can be costly, and 
production companies need to optimize their maintenance strategies to reduce costs while ensuring equipment reliability. In 
this context, optimization approaches involve determining the optimal time for maintenance activities and the best 
maintenance method to minimize costs while ensuring equipment reliability. Over the years, researchers have developed 
various optimization approaches to determine the optimal preventive action point that minimizes maintenance cost i1]. 
 
This study explores three optimization approaches for reducing maintenance costs: the age replacement model, optimal 
inspection model, and optimization via Design of Experiment (DOE). The age replacement model involves simulating the 
equipment's aging process and scheduling replacement when it reaches a certain age or usage level. The optimal inspection 
model aims to determine the best time to perform maintenance inspections to minimize costs while ensuring equipment 
reliability. The optimization via the DOE approach involves finding a mathematical model in order to minimize the total cost 
of preventive maintenance (PM) by using ANOVA analysis and response surface methodology to identify the optimal 
parameter values. Preventive maintenance is a crucial activity for industrial systems to ensure reliability, availability, and 
safety. PM involves regular inspections, repairs, and replacements to identify and address potential issues before they cause 
significant damage to the system. For production companies, minimizing maintenance costs is essential, and PM is often the 
dominant strategy. However, replacements and inspections may be necessary, leading to increased maintenance costs. Hence, 
determining the optimal preventive action point that minimizes maintenance cost is crucial.

This research paper explores three optimization approaches for this purpose. The first approach involves simulation 
using an age replacement model with a Weibull distribution, while the second approach is an optimal inspection model under 
the Weibull distribution function. The third approach is optimization via DOE, which uses ANOVA analysis and RSM to 
develop a mathematical model that corresponds to the minimum total cost through response optimization techniques. The 
common purpose of these two strategies is to determine the optimal replacement time that minimizes the expected cost. The 
age-replacement policy is one of the most common maintenance policies, where a system is replaced preventively at its age 
T or failure depending on which occurs first, assuming T is constant. [2] analyzed different characteristics of the age 
replacement model, and several studies have extended it to find the optimal expected cost [3-6] demonstrated how age 
replacement affects optimal replacement time and how the cost rate greatly increases when the policy is based on replacement 
costs with a small reactive-to-preventive cost ratio. Conversely, when replacement costs increase slowly and their ratio 
decreases in age, the cost rate decreases. [7] presented an extension of the age replacement model policy and block model, as 
well as a revised block replacement policy. [8] proposed a system in a series configuration with two units that are not identified 
based on the age replacement model and spare parts inventory to minimize the system's average maintenance cost per unit 
time. The age replacement model also deals with repairable series and parallel systems [9]. 
 
In addition to the age replacement model, an optimal inspection model under Weibull is also investigated. This model 
determines the optimal inspection time that minimizes the expected maintenance cost by taking into account the cost of 
inspection and the cost of failure. The model aims to find the optimal balance between these two costs. A mathematical model 
related to this maintenance preventive strategies has been described in the literature by [10]. The implementation of 
inspections in a maintenance program can come at a cost, and failure to control and optimize inspection intervals can result 
in high total inspection costs. Consequently, numerous studies have been performed in literature to address this issue. For 
instance, [11] presents a quasi-optimal inspection policy for leased equipment, aiming to minimize costs incurred by the lessor. 
The proposed model and algorithm determine the optimal inspection sequence, considering downtime penalties, inspection 
costs, lease period length, and equipment reliability. [12] proposed a framework for optimizing maintenance and inspection 
in wind energy systems. It reviewed existing literature, identified research gaps, and highlighted potential research directions. 
The third optimization approach investigated in this research is optimization via DOE. This approach is widely used in various 
fields, including reliability, maintenance, quality control, and social sciences. In recent years, DOE has also been applied to 
design optimization, where it helps to determine optimum values. Many studies have demonstrated the usefulness and 
effectiveness of DOE in solving optimization problems. For instance, [13] applied DOE to various fields and proved its 
effectiveness. DOE helps to study the impact of multiple variables on system response based on low-cost experiments to 
achieve reliable results, which aids in decision-making. For example, [14] applied DOE to investigate the main effects of 
minimized cost under the concept of production, maintenance, and quality control. Similarly, [15] applied DOE to PM and 
production decision to identify the significant factors influencing the total cost. 
 
The scientific contribution of this study is twofold. Firstly, it compares the effectiveness of three optimization approaches 
for reducing maintenance costs in preventive maintenance strategies. Secondly, it investigates the impact of the Weibull shape 
parameter and replacement time on the expected maintenance cost. The study provides valuable insights into the strengths 
and weaknesses of each optimization approach and the optimal approach depending on the specific system and operational 
context. The study's findings can assist production companies in selecting the most cost-effective preventive maintenance 
strategy for their industrial equipment, ultimately improving the overall reliability, availability, and safety of their systems. 
 
2. Optimization Approaches 
 
Optimization techniques are utilized in numerous research fields to obtain solutions that either maximize or minimize specific 
parameters of interest. The present study specifically concentrates on the minimization of maintenance costs for a particular 
system. Accordingly, three approaches are explored in this study: simulation-based techniques that employ age replacement, 
inspection models, and the design of experiments methodology.

2.3. Design of Experiment Model 
 
The Design of Experiments (DOE) is a statistical technique that involves conducting a sequence of experiments to investigate 
the impact of input variables (factors) on one or more output variables (response). The main objective of this study is to 
employ the DOE methodology to identify the key factors that affect the minimum total cost of the system. To achieve this 
goal, ANOVA analysis will be performed to determine the interaction of factors that significantly influence the anticipated 
cost, based on the values of factors and their corresponding responses. Figure 1 illustrates the methodology developed in this 
study. 
 
 
Figure 1. Structure of the proposed optimal approach 
 
The analysis will identify interval value factors that demonstrate insignificant differences in expected cost values and 
subsequently exclude them. Afterward, a mathematical model will be constructed based on significant factors using regression 
analysis and RSM methodology. The developed mathematical models will be optimized to identify optimal solutions and 
determine the combination of input variable settings that optimize the single response of the optimal cost.

5. Conclusion 
 
In this study, three optimization approaches for reducing maintenance costs were investigated: the age replacement model, 
optimal inspection model, and optimization via DOE. The study evaluated the performance of these approaches in terms of 
cost reduction under the Weibull distribution function. The first approach, the age replacement model, was based on a 
simulation that models the proposed strategy. A mathematical model expressing the expected total cost per unit was used to 

Optimization Approaches for Cost Reduction in Preventive Maintenance Strategies: A Comparative Study                        367 
check the conditions of existence and uniqueness of the optimal solution. The second approach was the inspection model, 
where a simulation was performed to determine the optimal inspection time corresponding to the minimal expected cost. The 
third approach was based on the design of experiments and statistical analysis. ANOVA analysis and response surface methods 
were used to develop a mathematical model that corresponded to optimal maintenance costs through response optimization 
techniques in terms of important decision-related factors and their interactions. The results showed that the Weibull shape 
parameter and replacement time significantly impacted the expected maintenance cost. Based on the findings, the age 
replacement model was found to be the most effective preventive maintenance strategy compared to the inspection model and 
design of the experiment for the Weibull distribution with various values of 𝛽. However, the age replacement model and 
design of experiment showed similar results when 𝛽 was equal to 3, 5, and 6, but not when 𝛽 was equal to 2. Therefore, the 
inspection model is the most expensive strategy among the three approaches for all values of 𝛽. Overall, this study provides 
valuable information to production companies looking to optimize their preventive maintenance strategies and reduce 
operational costs while remaining competitive in the market.
