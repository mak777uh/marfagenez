Abstract 
The demand for compressed air systems to power pneumatic tools has steadily increased across various industries, such as chemicals, 
construction, mining, and oil and gas. However, due to the high complexity of air compressor technology and the need for higher availability, 
standard maintenance activities are insufficient. As a result, compressed air systems require a proper high-level maintenance strategy and 
effective monitoring. This article addresses the imperfect preventive maintenance (PM) optimization problem by implementing preventive 
replacements and minimal repairs to maximize the average availability of the air compressor subject to continuous degradation while 
satisfying minimal maintenance cost rate requirements. Each maintenance action is associated with a cost and duration, and the imperfect 
PM and corrective maintenance results are modeled on the maintenance cost rate and availability. The proposed imperfect PM optimization 
model aims to determine the optimal number of imperfect repairs and the replacement time. A mathematical model is introduced as nonlinear 
programming, and an algorithm is proposed to solve the optimization problem. Overall, this article provides a comprehensive and formal 
analysis of the maintenance strategy for air compressors, offering valuable insights into the optimization of imperfect preventive 
maintenance. 
 
Keywords: availability; imperfect preventive maintenance; optimization; air-compressor; preventive replacement; mathematical modeling

1.  Introduction  
 
The air compressor station is a complex and comprehensive facility comprising an air receiver tank and air compressor that 
is vital to ensuring a continuous supply of compressed air for pneumatic tools in various industrial, trade, and workshop 
applications. Regular inspections and maintenance are necessary to ensure the equipment operates continuously and safely, 
and to prevent downtime. Various techniques and methods, such as RCM, FTA, RAM, and FMECA, are applied in developing 
maintenance strategies. Although maintenance actions help restore equipment to a near-new condition, maintenance costs 
increase incrementally over time. Therefore, a critical decision in the facility's lifecycle is to determine the lowest cost of 
maintenance or replacement. The decision to maintain or replace is challenging and crucial. As air compressors are subjected 
to continuous preventive maintenance and failure repair, their performance declines faster, and maintenance costs increase 
over time. The economic life cycle of the air-compressor under imperfect maintenance is crucial to maintenance management. 
How to increase air-compressor availability and make a decision on the economic replacement time is the main focus of this 
paper. This study will adopt the concepts of the maximization of availability combined with reliability analysis, in order to 
develop a replacement decision model for the air compressor under imperfect maintenance within cost constraints. 
 
Companies can achieve competitiveness in the market and profitability by ensuring high availability of their production 
units. This is achieved by developing and optimizing maintenance strategies for production systems. In the event of a failure, 
efficient maintenance plans are implemented to determine whether a repair or replacement action is necessary. Corrective 
maintenance (CM) and preventive maintenance (PM) are the two categories of maintenance systems that are considered for 
repair purposes. Corrective maintenance involves repairing faulty systems with the aim of restoring the broken systems. 
Preventive maintenance involves carrying out actions at regular intervals to minimize or avoid equipment failure and maintain

the required level of performance over a prescribed service life. Therefore, several studies suppose that preventive 
maintenance is perfect, which is an ideal situation between the effects (perfect, imperfect, and minimal) of maintenance.  
 
When dealing with complex reparable equipment, such as turbines in power plants, pumps, compressors, and pipelines, 
imperfect preventive maintenance actions and operations are often requested and adopted. These strategies aim to maintain 
equipment performance between the two extremes of operating states: the as-good-as-new state and the as-bad-as-old state 
[1]. Although imperfect maintenance is prevalent in practical maintenance activities, many studies have presented and used 
imperfect preventive maintenance strategies in different sectors. For instance, [2] presented optimization models for 
preventive maintenance under perfect and imperfect conditions, as well as two-level actions. [3] summarized imperfect 
maintenance models studied in literature from 1965 to 2020. The majority of imperfect maintenance strategies are based on 
the quasi-renewal process, with different models for modeling imperfect results, such as virtual age, the (p, q) model, and age 
reduction. [4] used reliability-centered maintenance models and hybrid hazard rate approaches to optimize imperfect 
preventive maintenance for multicomponent repair systems. [5] studied the availability and optimal imperfect maintenance 
strategies of a series configuration system and presented eleven imperfect repair maintenance models in their research results. 
A model was constructed to enhance the mission reliability of complex systems by considering varied maintenance behaviors 
such as minimal, imperfect, and replacement, and a selective maintenance decision-making model was proposed to maximize 
current reliability, as demonstrated in a case study. [6] investigated the analysis of availability and optimization of the 
maintenance of the maintained system in many imperfect repairs. 
 
Optimization is the process of achieving the best possible outcome within specific conditions. Its main objective is to 
determine the conditions that yield the highest or lowest value of certain decision variables in a function. In the design, 
construction, commissioning, and maintenance of any production system, many managerial and technological decisions must 
be made to maximize benefits or minimize effort. One critical objective in maintenance decisions is to optimize equipment 
availability, which ensures that the production system remains efficient while reducing maintenance costs. For example, 
maximizing equipment availability is crucial in pneumatic systems, control systems, and instrumentation, especially in 
automated industrial processes, where system downtime can have severe consequences. Numerous studies in the literature 
address the optimization of availability under imperfect preventive maintenance. [7] used a numerical algorithm and PSO to 
obtain an optimal imperfect preventive maintenance interval that maximizes the two-dimensional warranty product 
availability. [8] developed a preventive maintenance policy for a single system subject to random failure. The system is either 
periodically and imperfectly repaired with probability q or perfectly with probability p. They developed a mathematical model 
to determine the optimal preventive maintenance period that maximizes the system's stationary availability. [9] discussed an 
availability-based optimization model where imperfect preventive maintenance is applied under the hybrid hazard rate. [10] 
studied the optimization of reliability, availability, and cost of a system in parallel configuration under replacement and 
imperfect preventive maintenance. In [11], an integrated availability maximization algorithm was used to select one of four 
turbine components that exhibit decreased reliability. Imperfect maintenance was then applied to replace the failed turbine 
component. 
 
The purpose of this paper is to maximize the availability of an air compressor subject to continuous degradation under 
imperfect preventive maintenance with cost constraints. Air compressors are complex equipment used in various industries, 
and their proper maintenance is critical to ensure operational efficiency, reliability, and production output. Previous studies 
have investigated the optimization of compressor operations and maintenance, such as [12] and [13], demonstrating that 
optimizing maintenance and operational tasks leads to better solutions in terms of total costs. Recently, [14] studied new 
predictive maintenance approaches under Industry 4.0 for a centrifugal compressor. To achieve the objective of this paper, 
the number of imperfect repairs and preventive replacement time for the air compressor station will be determined to maximize 
availability while considering a limited budget. To this end, the current preventive maintenance interval will be adjusted 
within the maintenance cost by defining a maximum number of imperfect corrective maintenance events between two 
consecutive preventive maintenance activities. The paper's contribution is summarized as follows: 
• Investigating an imperfect maintenance model based on decision variables (number of imperfect repairs and 
replacement time). 
• Deriving air compressor station equipment availability and valuable time distributions.  
• Developing a model to minimize cost. 
• Designing an optimization problem to obtain the optimal decision variables that maximize availability while 
considering maintenance cost rate constraints. 
 
2. Imperfect Maintenance Model 
 
The imperfect maintenance model is a type of maintenance strategy that recognizes that despite regular maintenance efforts, 
there is still a chance that equipment or machinery will experience failures or breakdowns. This model assumes that

maintenance activities can only reduce the probability of failure and not eliminate it completely. The imperfect maintenance 
model aims to optimize the maintenance schedule to minimize downtime and repair costs while maintaining a certain level of 
reliability. This model takes into account factors such as equipment age, usage, and operating conditions to determine the best 
maintenance strategy to implement. The imperfect maintenance model is commonly used in industries such as manufacturing, 
transportation, and aviation where equipment reliability is crucial for efficient operations. The imperfect maintenance 
approach encompasses two main categories of maintenance, namely corrective and preventive maintenance. It is characterized 
by the degree to which an item's operational state is restored. Figure 1 provides a summary of this classification.

The imperfect maintenance model has been proposed for the purpose of modeling the performance, optimizing costs, and

• The system initiates operation at time 0. 
• The strategy's efficacy is evaluated over an infinite horizon. 
• Preventive replacement costs are presumed to be less than corrective replacement costs. 
• The failure rate is considered to be continuous and monotonically increasing

The selection of the repair time T and the number K of imperfect repair preventive maintenance (PM) actions can 
significantly impact the performance of the maintenance policy in economic terms. Specifically, a reduced number of 
imperfect repairs implies prolonged and uninterrupted usage of the system, but at the expense of an increased risk of failure. 
 
On the other hand, an increased number of imperfect repair PM actions reduces the number of system failures, but leads 
to higher maintenance costs and system unavailability. The maintenance optimization problem addressed in this paper is to 
identify a balance between the number of imperfect repair PM actions and the minimum cost required to maximize the average 
system availability. 
 
2.3. Maintenance optimization model 
 
In the present paper, the preventive maintenance strategy adopted involves replacing the system either after the (K-1)th repair 
preventive maintenance or after a certain age, whichever occurs first. Corrective maintenance is carried out when the system 
fails, while preventive maintenance is conducted when the system survives until reaching a certain age T. The repair action is 
imperfect, meaning that the lifetime of the system after each repair is reduced to a fraction of its previous lifetime, thereby 
representing a decreasing quasi-renewal process. Additionally, the repair time is increased to several times the previous one, 
and the successive lifetimes and times are independent, thereby representing a growing quasi-renewal process. As such, T and 
K are both considered decision variables and parameters. The optimal maintenance policy involves determining the optimum 
values for both the number of repair K and the replacement time T that minimize the expected total cost and subsequently

developing a more effective and efficient maintenance strategy for the air compressor system equipment. 
 
4. Conclusion 
 
Optimizing system availability while minimizing maintenance costs is a challenging yet critical objective in the engineering 
field. One of the most significant factors contributing to the prevention of system failures is the implementation of an effective 
preventive maintenance strategy. This paper addresses the optimization of imperfect preventive maintenance models, where 
repair actions are subject to imperfections modeled under a quasi-renewal process. The model takes into account both 
availability and expected maintenance costs, from which a mathematical optimization model is derived. Through investigation, 
optimal maintenance policies are derived, which include determining the replacement time and the number of imperfect 
repairs required to maximize the average availability of the system while adhering to minimal maintenance cost constraints. 
To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed model, an air compressor system example is continuously monitored and

subject to continuous degradation. The study shows that the proposed model can optimize system availability while 
minimizing maintenance costs. There are several potential extensions of this work, such as incorporating the use of resources 
and factors related to component quality in future studies.
