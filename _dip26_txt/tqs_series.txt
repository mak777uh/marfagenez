Optimal Age-replacement Policy for Series Systems 
Considering Maintenance Time 
 
Kentaro Fujioka*1, Lu Jin2 
 
Department of Informatics, University of Electro-Communications,  
1-5-1 Chofugaoka, Chofu, Tokyo, 182-8585 Japan. 
 
1fio-kent0536@sixth.inf.uec.ac.jp, 2jin.lu@uec.ac.jp 
 
 
Abstract: 
Age-replacement is one of the most commonly used maintenance policies based on preventive action to prevent 
system failure. In many age-replacement settings, it is assumed that the maintenance time can be ignored since it 
is short compared with operation time. However, maintenance, especially corrective maintenance, may take a non-
negligible amount of time due to various factors such as ordering replacement parts, equipping the maintenance 
team, and transporting the maintenance equipment. Neglecting maintenance time when optimizing maintenance 
policies for multi-unit systems may thus lead to sub-optimal or even incorrect solutions to the problem, resulting 
in higher maintenance costs. We present an optimal age -replacement policy for systems consisting of multiple 
independent units connected in series. The failure of any one of the units causes the entire system to fail, which is 
immediately detected. We formulated a model that integrates both preventive and corrective maintenance times  
for determining an optimal preventive replacement interval, minimizing the expected long run cost per unit time. 
The sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of the corresponding optimal solution are derived. The 
performance of the proposed maintenance policy was evaluated by comparing it with a conventional one that does 
not tak e into account maintenance time. N umerical examples  illustrate the effects of downtime cost and 
maintenance time on the proposed maintenance policy. 
 
Keywords 
Corrective Maintenance, Cost Rate, Optimal Policy, Preventive Maintenance, Preventive Replacement Interval 
 
 
1. Introduction 
 
Our lives are supported by a wide variety of systems  that incorporate various  technologies and products. 
Preventive maintenance of these systems is important because their failure may cause disruption in our daily lives. 
Maintenance is usually performed  on a regular basis  to ensure system reliability. There are two basic types of 
maintenance, preventive and corrective , depending on whether the system is maintained before or after it fails. 
Corrective maintenance can be costly, so it is usually better to carry out preventive maintenance. However, frequent 
preventive maintenance is no t cost-effective. This has led to the formulation  of many policies integrating 
preventive and corrective maintenance,  including age-replacement maintenance and condition- monitoring 
maintenance. We focus on  the former, as it is one of the most commonly used maintenance policies  based on 
preventive action to prevent system failure. 
Age-replacement means that a system  is replaced at failure or at a specified preventive replacement interval , 
whichever occurs first. The interval that minimizes the m aintenance cost per unit time is considered the  optimal 
interval. Evans and Castro  (2008) considered an age -replacement policy with minimal repair for systems  that 
experience two causes of failure, obtained the condition for the existence of an optimal replacement interval, and 
proposed an optimal age-replacement policy under certain constraints. Peng et al. (2010)  proposed an optimal 
policy that minimizes the maintenance cost of a system that can experience a series of failures due to degradation 
and random shocks. The models of Evans and Castro  (2008) and Peng et al. (2010)  can be considered two -unit 
systems, and more generalized models can be considered  multi-unit systems. Shafiee and Finkelstein  (2015)

It was assumed in most previous  studies that maintenance takes a negligible  amount of time compared with 
operation time. However, maintenance, especially  corrective maintenance, may take a non -negligible amount of 
time due to variance factors. For example, maintenance of offshore wind power may require transporting 
equipment and material and dispatching repair personnel to the site. Maintenance time in such cases can therefore 
not be ignored. 
Many studies have considered maintenance time for single-unit systems. The age-replacement policy developed 
by Barlow and Hunter (1960) is an early example. Xie and Cui (2005) and Chen et al. (2019) investigated methods 
for evaluating system availability. Sanoubar et al. (2020) numerically evaluated an age- replacement policy with 
minimal repairs that takes into account deviation of  the actual maintenance time from the planned time and 
considered the effects on costs caused by not maintaining the system as planned. Cavalcante et al. (2021) proposed 
a hybrid policy of inspection and replacement with a fixed periodic schedule and takes into account the lag between 
time of failure and time of maintenance as the downtime cost. 
We focused on series systems consisting of different types of units with the aim of examining the conditions for 
the existence of an optimal replacement interval  and optimal solution when the loss due to maintenance time  is 
taken into account. The main contributions of this paper  are fourfold. 1) We propose an age-replacement policy 
for systems consisting of multiple types of independent units. 2) On the basis of the proposed policy , a 
maintenance-optimization model that takes into account the various maintenance times due to the different types 
of units is formulated for use in determining the optimal preventive replacement interval. 3) Sufficient conditions 
for the existence and uniqueness of the corresponding optimal solution are then derived. 4) Comparison of the 
performance of the proposed policy with one that does not take into account maintenance time  indicates that the 
former is superior in cost-effectiveness. 
This paper is structured as follows. Section 2 describes the series system model considered in this study. Section 
3 describes our proposed optimal age-replacement policy for this system and the conditions for the existence and 
uniqueness of the optimal replacement interval . Section 4 describes the performance of the proposed policy and 
the effects of downtime cost and maintenance time on the proposed policy.  Section 5 summarizes the key points  
and points out the limitations of this study.

2.2 Maintenance Actions and Times 
We consider two maintenance actions: scheduled preventive replacement at interval 𝑇𝑇  and corrective 
maintenance at system failure, which occurs during 𝑇𝑇. The probability distribution of the time taken for preventive 
replacement 𝑌𝑌𝑃𝑃  has cumulative distribution function 𝐺𝐺𝑃𝑃(𝑦𝑦) . If a system failure occurs during 𝑇𝑇 , corrective 
maintenance takes time 𝑌𝑌𝑖𝑖, which depends on  the failed unit 𝑖𝑖. 𝑌𝑌𝑖𝑖 is distributed with a probability distribution 
with cumulative distribution function 𝐺𝐺𝑖𝑖(𝑦𝑦) . Both preventive replacement and corrective maintenance are 
assumed to be perfect in that the system is restored to normal operation.  This assumption is same as  previous 
studies (Wang and Pham (2005); Shafiee and Finkelstein (2015)).

4.4 Discussion 
From the results presented above , we can see that the proposed age -replacement policy achieved better 
performance in terms of Indicators 1 and 2 than a conventional one. For systems having longer failure times, longer 
maintenance times, higher corrective maintenance costs, or larger downtime losses, better performance can be 
attained in terms of Indicator 1. This finding of costs matches that of Shafiee and Finkelstein (2015). Similarly, for 
systems having longer maintenance times or larger downtime losses, better performance can be attained in terms 
of Indicator 2. However, longer failure times and higher corrective maintenance costs lead to Indicator 2 showing 
the opposite trend than Indicator 1. This means that the age-replacement policy should be chosen on the basis of 
the system properties. Furthermore, as discussed in  Section 4.3, various factors affect the optimal interval. The 
results obtained here should be helpful in constructing effective age-replacement policies.
