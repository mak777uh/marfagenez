Abstract. In practice, at some instances, the repair of a failed item can be unsuccessful and should 
be repeated due to various reasons. This can happen in practice with a minimal repair when a failed 
part of a system is replaced, e.g., by the faulty spare.  The corresponding stochastic model that 
takes into account this possibility can be described probabilistically in terms of the Polya-Aeppli 
process, which is a generalization of the Poisson process to the case of multiple occurrences of 
events. Some useful properties for considering optimal preventive maintenance problems are 
derived. The optimal replacement policy for a system with the multi-attempt minimal repairs is 
defined and analysed. The characteristic feature of this policy is that the system is replaced not 
only on reaching the predetermined age but also after a fixed number of unsuccessful minimal 
repairs. The detailed numerical examples illustrate our findings. 
Keywords: Minimal repair; Polya-Aeppli process; Poisson process; maintenance; optimal 
replacement 
 
1. Introduction 
Minimal repair was first introduced by Barlow and Hunter (1960) and, since that time, it became 
the most often used type of repair in maintenance modelling. In contrast to perfect repair (as good 
as new), it is an “as bad as old” repair, bringing a system to a state it had just before a failure (Cha 
and Finkelstein (2018a), Chahkandi et al (2014), Finkelstein (2008) to name a few). The most 
common situation in practice described by a minimal repair is when a failure of a system that 
consists of a large number of components is repaired by replacing only the failed component.

failure rate and the survival function, respectively. It follows from the definition (Finkelstein 
(2008)) that an item that has started operation at 𝑡 = 0 , had failed and was instantaneously

     In practice, at some instances, minimal repair can be unsuccessful and it should be repeated.  
This can happen, e.g., due to the faulty spare parts, the error in diagnostics of the failed 
components, human error, the failure of another component while replacing the failed one, the 
design fault, an impact of external or internal shocks, etc. Even in the everyday life, when one tries 
to restore the failed home appliance, this does not always happen from the first attempt (the 
simplest example would be the light bulb) (Cha and Finkelstein (2023). Therefore, to model the 
real-life minimal maintenance of various engineering systems, the possibility of an unsuccessful 
minimal repair should be considered.  
     The process of minimal (instantaneous) repairs is the Poisson point process, which is orderly 
(without multiple occurrences), whereas the described situation with multi-attempt minimal 
repairs suggests that the process to be employed for modelling should be non-orderly, i.e., with 
the possibility of multiple occurrences.  For the corresponding modelling, in our study, we suggest 
to use the Polya-Aeppli process, which is a generalization of the Poisson process to the case of 
multiple occurrences (Minkova (2004),  Jacob and Jose (2018)). This process is based on the well-
known Polya-Aeppli distribution, which is a compound-type generalization of the Poisson 
distribution (see, e.g., Johnson et al (2005)). The authors in the referenced papers consider only 
actuarial applications of this process to obtain, e.g., the corresponding ruin probabilities when 
claims arrive in patches. Moreover, practically all publications (except Chukova and Minkova 
(2019)) deal with homogeneous settings when inter-arrival times in the process are exponentially 
distributed (HPP). However, preventive maintenance (PM), which is the application considered in 
our paper, in this case cannot be defined, as there is no related ‘aging’ in items and, therefore, PM 
is, obviously, irrelevant. The PM modelling is one of the most vital topics in reliability theory and 
practice. Several books entirely devoted to this topic (see, e.g. Nakagawa (2005), Gertsbakh 
(2005), Wang and Pham (2006)) and thousands of papers can be found in reliability literature (see 
e.g.,  Finkelstein et al (2023), Guo and Liang (2022), Hamidi et al (2016), Pedersen and Vatn 
(2022), Tambe and Kulkarni (2022), Zhang et al (2021), Wang et al (2021) among others for recent 
developments). However, all these studies do not consider the topic of the current paper. 
     In our recent paper (Cha and Finkelstein (2023)), the novel notion of the multi-attempt minimal 
repair was introduced for the first time in the literature. The main emphasis was on considering 
the setting when the probability of unsuccessful repair is increasing in time, thus introducing 
another source of aging (along with the increasing failure rate) in the model. The latter is important 
for the corresponding PM modelling.  The number of unsuccessful minimal repairs was considered 
as unlimited. However, from practical considerations and in the framework of preventive 
maintenance, it is natural to assume that if the unsuccessful repair is performed several times, then

    The developed approach and methodology are innovative as well as the application to the 
described PM problem. We assume that this publication can trigger research on various 
applications of non-orderly stochastic processes (with multiple occurrences). For instance, the 
cascading shock processes can be another topic of research in this area.  
     The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we provide the definitions and properties of 
the non-stationary Polya-Aeppli process which are necessary for the forthcoming discussion. 
Section 3 provides theoretical description of the ingredients of the corresponding optimization 
problem, which is formulated and discussed. Section 4 contains numerical illustrations for the 
obtained results. Short concluding remarks are given in Section 5. Finally, a possible 
generalization to the case when the probability of unsuccessful event is indexed by a random 
parameter is deferred to Appendix. 
       
2. Preliminaries 
Let a system, characterized by the baseline failure rate 𝜆(𝑡), be minimally (instantaneously) 
repaired on each failure. Each minimal repair is unsuccessful with probability (1 − 𝜃) and is 
successful with probability 𝜃. If it is unsuccessful, it should be repeated. For the preliminary results 
of this section that are necessary for our further reasoning, as in Cha and Finkelstein (2023), we 
assume that according to the stated probabilities, the number of unsuccessful repairs before the 
successful one is a geometric random variable (therefore, with unbounded support). However, in 
practice, one should not perform unsuccessful repairs ‘unlimited’ and a reasonable bound after 
which the system should be ‘preventively’ replaced should be considered. Possible causes for 
unsuccessful minimal repairs were listed in the Introduction.

5. Concluding remarks 
In practice, minimal repair can be unsuccessful, in which case it should be repeated until an item 
is operable. This occurs due to various reasons: the faulty spare parts, the error in diagnostics of 
the failed component, the maintenance action fault, the failure of another component while 
replacing the failed one, the design fault, etc.  
The Polya-Aeppli process is a convenient tool for modelling the Poisson-driven processes with 
multiple occurrences.  The latter occur, for instance, in the case of unsuccessful minimal repairs.   
In the literature, the Polya-Aeppli process was used mostly in risk and actuarial settings describing 
e.g., the possibility of multiple claims in a small interval of time. Some other reliability 
applications can be considered in the future research, e.g., in modelling of cascading shocks that 
are impacting operating systems. On the other hand, the cascading failures in systems can be also 
considered, when a failure of one component triggers the failures of several others. 
In Section 2, we have obtained some useful properties of the Polya-Aeppli process that were 
used in the rest of the paper for the corresponding preventive maintenance modelling. From 
practical considerations and in the framework of preventive maintenance, it is natural to assume 
that if the unsuccessful repair is performed several times, then it is reasonable to replace a system 
and not continue with costly minimal repairs. This can indicate that something ‘is wrong’ with a 
system and it should be replaced. Therefore, in this paper we mostly consider the case when the 
possible number of unsuccessful repairs is limited. 
The optimal PM problem that minimizes the expected long-run cost rate for a system with a 
limited number of unsuccessful minimal repairs is discussed. Its numerical analysis is presented 
showing that for the considered values of parameters the value of k=1 achieves the minimal cost 
rate as compared with larger values.
