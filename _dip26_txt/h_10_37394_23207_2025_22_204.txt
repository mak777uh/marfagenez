studied
two PM models which were a failure rate

2 Problem Description
When a operating system experiences an unexpected
failure,wegenerallyintendtohaveitrepaired. When
repair takes so long time that the downtime is highly
necessary to be considered as the cost of the system,
particularly in peak seasons. When repair time
exceeds tolerable limits, an alternative method is to
replacethesystemtomeettheincreasingoperational
demands.
We consider a one-unit system constrained
by random failures and subsequent repairs which
undergoes immediate repair each time a failure
occurs, if it is repaired in limit time T0, it will return
totheoperatingstate.Anassumptionismadethatthe
lifetime of a unit is ξ and the failure distribution is a
continuousdistribution F (t) whichhasafinitemean
µ. Letussupposethemaintenancetimeofaunitthat
follows the general distribution G1(t)is represented
by η1, and its mean is β1..The repair time of a unit
is η1 and define the repair distribution G1(t) with a
finite mean β1. Furthermore, it is assumed that the
restored unit is functionally equivalent to a new one,
enabling immediate recommencement of operations
withoutperformancedegradation.
After the unit has operated for the planned time
T without experiencing any failure, the unit will
be stopped for preventive replacement to avoid
unexpected accidents. The time for replacement of
a unit is η2, and its distribution is postulated to be a
general distribution G2(t) which has a finite mean
β2. For the purpose of analyzing the problem, The
statesoftheunitaredefinedasfollows.
