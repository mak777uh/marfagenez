Abstract. Preventive maintenance (PM) has played an increasingly important role in 
manufacturing. Many PM strategies have been proposed to determine optimal decisions 
with the assumption of perfect PM, which is seldom happened in reality. Besides, a great 
many studies have mainly considered single-objective optimizations instead of multi-
objective issues. In this paper, we propose a model simultaneously determining spare part 
numbers and imperfect PM intervals based on multi-attribute utility theory (MAUT), one of 
the multi-objective decision making theories. Reliability, cost, and supply risk are seen as 
three main conflicting attributes. A numerical application is illustrated based on the data 
gathered from a steel firm. Sensitivity analysis is carried out to show the impact of 
parameters. The conclusion is shown that the model consists of facts. 
1. Introduction 
Preventive maintenance (PM) is an effective way to reduce the cost and improve the reliability of 
production equipment [1-2]. Deteriorations, erosion as well as failure are subject to their usage and 
age, which cause higher production costs and lower product output [3]. PM activities are usually 
performed based on the deterioration process monitoring and prediction. 
Models using traditional PM policies assumed that the systems or equipment become as good as 
new after PM activities. Yang proposed a three-stage failure process model with perfect PM using 
the age-based replacement policy to obtain an optimization [4]. Wang proposed several perfect 
maintenance models using the delay-time concept, such as a block-based inspection model that uses 
a recursive algorithm to optimize the inspection interval [5]. Xu tackled issues of availability 
optimization for repairable systems using both corrective and perfect preventive maintenance [6]. 
According to Ferreira [7], he studied a decision model to determine perfect PM intervals with 
minimum cost based on condition monitoring. 
However, perfect PM does not conform to the fact. Systems may be as bad as before or become 
a little worse than the new after PM because of improper adjustments, bad parts and damage done 
during PM activities [8]. Issues of imperfect PM have drawn a lot of attention and a great many 
studies have been carried out. In Zhang’s paper, a two-component system with failure interactions 
was considered. It was assumed that Component 1 performed imperfect corrective maintenance and

Component 2 was perfectly repaired. [9] According to Mabrouk et al.’s research [10], imperfect PM 
was executed when the equipment reliability reached a certain threshold. An optimal reliability 
threshold as well as an optimal PM interval was calculated. 
Many studies have been focused on the aspect of the whole production planning. For example, in 
GouiaaMtibaa’s study [11], the number of produced batches and the number of imperfect PM 
actions were two decision variables to be determined. Liao et al. [12] showed an optimization model 
based on economic production quantity policy. An optimal total cost was achieved with imperfect 
maintenance. 
 Spare parts inventory management is one of the main research areas. Kennedy gave an overview 
of recent literature on spare parts inventories. It may be feasible to order parts just in time instead of 
stock. For the unplanned repairs, the safety stock policy is necessary [13]. His paper discussed 
different situations of spare parts inventories: multi-echelon problems, repairable spare parts, age-
based replacement and so on. In W. Wang’s paper, a joint optimization for spare parts inventory and 
the preventive maintenance inspection interval was represented. The model developed a delay-time 
concept in the situation of perfect PM [14]. According to Ighravwe [15], he summarized that a 
combination of spare part availability, maintenance strategy, equipment availability and 
breakdowns of equipment should be established in order to remove bottlenecks on production lines.  
It can be observed in the researches above that most of the models considered single-objective 
optimizations. Different decision-making objectives may result in different PM strategies. Multi-
criteria decision making is commonly used in many research areas and makes it possible to trade off 
among different objectives and practical issues.  However， only a few papers have studied PM 
from multi-objective aspects [16]. Almeida [17] proposed a Bayesian approach for two maintenance 
problems decision: repair contract selection and spares provisioning.  
Studies discussed PM issues with multi-attribute strategies seldom considered spare parts 
inventories and PM simultaneously [18-22]. Multi-attribute utility theory (MAUT) as a branch of 
multi-criteria decision making, can visualize essential aspects simultaneously [23]. This paper is 
different from previous studies. The novelty of our work lies in the simultaneous determination of 
spare part numbers and imperfect PM intervals using one of the multi-attribute strategies. Decision-
makers’ preferences are taken into account based on MAUT. Reliability, cost, and supply risk are 
attributes. The numbers of spare parts and imperfect PM intervals are the decision variables.  
The remaining parts of this paper are structured as follows. Section 2 provides the problem 
description and notation. Section 3 propose the decision model, which is formulated based on the 
utility functions of cost, risk, and reliability respectively.  Section 4 shows experiments and results 
to illustrate the practicability of the model. Section 5 makes a discussion and concludes that the 
multi-criteria decision model we conducted is applicable in optimizing imperfect maintenance 
intervals and the number of spare parts.

2.1. Description 
In this paper, we consider a single-unit system or equipment. The system has the same failure rate 
after minor PM as it had before with the probability of

probability
q . If a failure occurs, a replacement is performed. PM actions can take place at any time 
during operation. Since PM is always carried out during a period, such as one day, two weeks, the 
action space is defined as a discrete set of alternatives. Utilities of attributes are considered to be 
additive independent [24]. Spare parts can be ordered immediately when a shortage occurs. 
However, the prices in the planning horizon are lower than the prices we purchase during the 
operation. The system or equipment can be renewed by failure replacement or perfect PM.

attitudes towards reliability and supply risk are risk-averse. This assumption is in agreement with 
the fact. Decision-makers want to ensure that the reliability of the system remains at a 
relatively high level and to mitigate risk to a specified level with an acceptable spends.

In this paper, we consider a single- unit system or equipment. Minor PM or perfect PM can be 
done during the operation. The parameter 
  shows the probability of having spare parts, which is 
determined by numbers of spare parts and PM intervals. Cost, reliability, and supply risk are the 
three main attributes considered. The decision-makers’ attitudes towards reliability and supply risk 
are risk-averse. Compared with the above two attributes, the attitude of decision-makers related to 
cost is risk-neutral. The optimization is determined from different perspectives instead of 
considering ‘cost’ as the only decision-making objective. Moreover, it can determine both spare part 
numbers and PM intervals simultaneously. In the concluding section, we analysed the sensitivity of 
parameters. Further work may be undertaken using dynamics approaches based on condition-based 
PM methods.
