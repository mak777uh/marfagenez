The hydrogel achieved loading efficiencies of 85.2 ± 2.5% for ampicillin and 78.6 ± 1.2% for TGF-β 1 , with optimized swelling properties and mechanical characteristics suitable for soft tissue engineering. ## Introduction 
 1 The complex pathology of these wounds involves persistent bacterial colonization, sustained inflammatory response, and impaired cellular regenerative capacity, creating a microenvironment that resists conventional therapeutic interventions. 2 Traditional wound dressings primarily serve as passive physical barriers, lacking the capability to simultaneously address infection control and tissue regeneration. Even advanced single-function scaffolds that target either antimicrobial protection or tissue regeneration individually often prove insufficient, necessitating the development of multifunctional biomaterial platforms capable of orchestrating both therapeutic functions. 3,4 
 5 The high water content and three-dimensional porous architecture closely mimic the native extracellular matrix, providing an ideal microenvironment for cell migration, proliferation, and tissue ingrowth. 6 Among various hydrogel systems, polyphosphate-based materials have attracted considerable attention due to their intrinsic biocompatibility and bioactivity. Inorganic polyphosphate (polyP), a naturally occurring component of bone matrix, participates in critical biological processes including biomineralization, energy metabolism, and intracellular signaling. 7 Notably, polyP can form coacervate structures through multivalent cation interactions, providing a versatile platform for drug delivery and tissue engineering applications. 8 These coacervate networks offer sustained release capabilities while maintaining the metabolic energy supply essential for cellular repair processes. 
 9,10 The combination of polyphosphate and alginate is expected to synergistically enhance structural integrity while maintaining the bioactive functions of polyP. To achieve comprehensive wound healing, the co-delivery of therapeutic agents addressing both infection control and tissue regeneration is essential. Ampicillin, a broad-spectrum β-lactam antibiotic, provides potent bactericidal activity against common wound pathogens, while transforming growth factor-β 1 (TGF-β 1 ) serves as a master regulator of tissue repair by promoting fibroblast proliferation, collagen synthesis, and inflammatory resolution. 11

Through systematic in vitro evaluations, the antibacterial performance, effects on dermal cell growth, anti-senescence efficacy, and wound healing outcomes were comprehensively investigated. 2.

 In vitro degradation was evaluated by incubating pre-weighed freeze-dried hydrogel samples in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) at 37 °C with gentle shaking (80 rpm) over 14 days. At predetermined time points (days 1, 3, 7 and 14), samples were retrieved, freeze-dried, and weighed. ## Enzymatic synthesis and antibacterial efficacy of ampicillin 
 E. coli ATCC 25922 was 4 µg mL −1 , which aligns with the typical MIC range of commercial ampicillin against this strain and meets the EUCAST susceptibility criterion for Enterobacterales (≤8 µg mL −1 ), confirming that the synthesized product retains potent antibacterial activity against susceptible bacteria. 
 E. coli ATCC 25922 as shown in Fig. 1A . At 8× MIC (32 µg mL −1 ), the bacterial load decreased by 3.2 log 10  CFU mL −1 within 6 hours and by 4.2 log 10  CFU mL −1 after 24 hours, satisfying the standard definition of bactericidal activity which requires at least a 3 log 10  CFU mL −1 reduction within 24 hours. At lower concentrations ranging from 0.5× to 2× MIC, bacteriostatic effects were observed with only minimal reductions in CFU mL −1 . Extracellular DNA release levels were closely correlated with the bactericidal activity as shown in Fig. 1B . ## Bioactivity verification and anti-aging efficacy of recombinant human TGF-β 1 
 1 demonstrated potent proliferative effects on human dermal fibroblasts in a dose-dependent and time-dependent manner as shown in Fig. 2 . Under serum-starved conditions, TGF-β 1 concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 10 ng mL −1 progressively enhanced cell proliferation across all time points examined. At 72 hours, the 0.5 ng mL −1 concentration achieved 38.2% enhancement over the negative control, while 1 ng mL −1 reached 52.6% enhancement, satisfying the pre-defined criterion for the optimal concentration. Consequently, 1 ng mL −1 TGF-β 1 was selected as the working concentration for subsequent anti-aging and hydrogel loading experiments, providing robust proliferative activity while minimizing reagent consumption. Groups C and E, containing ampicillin-loaded hydrogels, demonstrated potent bactericidal activity, reducing bacterial load from the initial 5.70 log 10  CFU mL −1 to 2.45 and 2.15 log 10  CFU mL −1 at 24 hours, respectively. ## Discussion

The co-loaded hydrogel achieved encapsulation efficiencies of 85.2 ± 2.5% for ampicillin and 78.6 ± 1.2% for TGF-β 1 , demonstrating superior loading performance compared to conventional drug-loaded hydrogel systems. 17 The zinc ion-mediated crosslinking strategy created a stable coacervate network that effectively retained both therapeutic agents while permitting sustained release over extended periods.

This potent activity against Escherichia coli aligns with findings from previous antibiotic-releasing biomaterial studies. 18 Notably, the superior antibacterial effect of the co-loaded hydrogel compared to ampicillin alone suggests potential synergistic interactions, possibly attributable to enhanced drug release kinetics or improved bacterial membrane penetration mediated by polyphosphate components. 19 The proliferative effects on human dermal fibroblasts reached 222.5% at 96 hours, substantially exceeding those reported in previous TGF-β 1 -loaded hydrogel studies. 20,21 The anti-senescence efficacy, characterized by a reduction in SA-β-gal-positive cells to 24.2%, was comparable to established antioxidant treatments. This effect is particularly relevant for chronic wound healing, where oxidative stress-induced cellular senescence impairs tissue regeneration. 13 
 1 act on complementary anti-inflammatory and pro-regenerative axes. Ampicillin eliminates the bacterial triggers of inflammation by inhibiting cell-wall synthesis through inactivation of penicillin-binding proteins, thereby removing the pathogen-associated molecular patterns that activate Toll-like receptor signaling and sustain pro-inflammatory cytokine release. Concurrently, TGF-β 1 promotes inflammatory resolution by inhibiting NF-κB signaling via upregulation of IκBα and by driving M2 macrophage polarization, shifting the wound microenvironment from a pro-inflammatory to a pro-regenerative state. 22–24 Second, the co-loaded hydrogel showed a 0.30 log 10  CFU mL −1 additional reduction at 24 h relative to ampicillin alone (Group E 2.15 ± 0.10 vs. Group C 2.45 ± 0.12). This modest but reproducible enhancement is consistent with two non-mutually-exclusive mechanisms: TGF-β 1 , as a ∼25 kDa growth factor, may partially displace ampicillin from polyphosphate binding sites through competitive ionic interactions, modestly accelerating ampicillin release and increasing the local concentration at the bacterial surface; and the TGF-β 1 – induced conformational rearrangement of the polyphosphate network may enlarge the mesh size, facilitating ampicillin diffusion and improving bacterial membrane penetration. Similar co-delivery synergies between antimicrobial agents and growth factors have been reported in multifunctional wound dressings, supporting the generalizability of this strategy. Third, in the cell–bacteria co-culture model, only the co-loaded formulation maintained fibroblast viability above 100% throughout the 96 h observation, whereas ampicillin alone preserved 95.4% viability and the unloaded matrix failed to protect cells entirely (38.5% at 96 h). Notably, the co-loaded group also showed the most favorable anti-senescence profile, indicating that the combined effects of ampicillin and TGF-β 1 extend beyond antibacterial and proliferative functions to encompass cellular aging regulation. This sequential mechanism – rapid bacterial clearance in the first 24 h creating a permissive window for TGF-β1-driven proliferation – cannot be replicated by sequential application of separate formulations and is uniquely enabled by co-delivery from a single scaffold.

Though weaker than ampicillin-mediated bactericidal performance, this inherent antibacterial activity originates from two synergistic mechanisms of polyphosphate anions: sequestration of Mg 2+ and Ca 2+ required for bacterial membrane stability and enzyme activity, as well as electrostatic binding with bacterial surface components to disrupt membrane structure. 25,26 For infected wounds where antibiotics hardly maintain full bactericidal concentration in lesion tissues, such matrix activity effectively restrains bacterial reproduction and biofilm formation under sub-inhibitory antibiotic levels. Moreover, the matrix itself facilitates tissue regeneration. Without TGF-β 1 loading, unloaded polyP-HG elevated 96 h fibroblast proliferation to 162.8%, obviously higher than the 138.5% value of blank control. Cellular alkaline phosphatase can hydrolyze polyphosphate to produce inorganic phosphate and pyrophosphate, supplying ATP-like energy to fuel cell biosynthesis and proliferation during wound repair. In summary, the polyphosphate hydrogel acts as a multifunctional inherently bioactive scaffold. Its overall therapeutic performance stems from the synergistic effects of polyphosphate, zinc ions and alginate, rather than relying merely on the encapsulated functional agents. Collectively, the polyphosphate-crosslinked hydrogel serves as an intrinsically bioactive and multifunctional therapeutic platform. Its overall wound-healing efficacy relies on the integrated bioactivities of the polyphosphate-based matrix itself, rather than merely depending on the loaded ampicillin.

Five-cycle uniaxial compression tests demonstrated > 90% height recovery and stable hysteresis loops from the second cycle onwards, with energy dissipation ratios of 12–15% that are comparable to native dermal tissue responses. The 14 days degradation profile, with 65.4–71.5% mass remaining at 14 days, matches the expected clinical timeline for chronic wound healing. The clinical relevance of these scaffolds is further supported by their wound-dressing performance parameters: an exudate absorption capacity of 2.85–3.41 g g −1 , well above the 2.0 g g −1 threshold and comparable to commercial alginate dressings; water vapor transmission rates of 1854–1925 g per m 2 per day, approaching the ideal range of 2000–2500 g per m 2 per day required to maintain a moist wound environment that promotes re-epithelialization while preventing desiccation and maceration; and 14 days structural stability of 76.5–82.6% mass remaining, confirming sustained physical integrity and drug release over clinically relevant timescales. Collectively, these rheological, fatigue, degradation, and wound-dressing characteristics support the suitability of the polyphosphate-crosslinked hydrogel platform for clinical wound dressing applications, particularly in the management of chronic infected wounds where prolonged exudate management, moisture balance, and sustained therapeutic release are essential.

## Conclusions 
 1 achieved high encapsulation efficiencies (85.2 ± 2.5% and 78.6 ± 1.2%, respectively), demonstrating superior drug loading performance compared to conventional hydrogel systems. The resulting multifunctional scaffold exhibited significant synergistic therapeutic effects in vitro , including potent bactericidal activity (exceeding 4 log 10  CFU mL −1 reduction within 24 hours), enhanced human dermal fibroblast proliferation (222.5% at 96 hours), and effective anti-senescence efficacy (SA-β-gal-positive cells reduced to 24.2%). These findings demonstrate that the co-delivery approach successfully integrates bacterial clearance with tissue regeneration, providing a promising strategy for treating infected chronic wounds. The polyphosphate-crosslinked hydrogel platform shows potential for applications in advanced wound dressings, burn treatment, and soft tissue regeneration. Future directions should focus on in vivo validation to confirm therapeutic efficacy in animal models, systematic safety and immunogenicity assessments, optimization of scalable production processes, and investigation of performance against multidrug-resistant bacterial strains.
